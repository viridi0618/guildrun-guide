## directAnswer

Most Guildrun runs die because of the same five decisions. The most expensive mistakes are: over-rerolling the shop (3 rerolls = 6 Shards, enough for an item), never using Rush (leaving free Shards on the table), treating your reserve bench like a storage closet, buying items that don't match your hero class, and starting on too high a difficulty. Fix these and your runs will consistently go deeper. Each mistake is broken down below with the game data or community reports that support it.

### Mistake 1: Over-Rerolling the Shop

heading: Reroll Addiction — The Most Expensive Habit
body: |
  **The mistake**: Clicking reroll three or four times per shop because you didn't see exactly what you wanted.

  **What it costs**: Rerolls cost 1, 2, 3, 4... Shards each within a single shop. Three rerolls = 6 Shards. Four rerolls = 10 Shards. For comparison, that's enough to buy a hero or an item outright — and a guaranteed purchase is always worth more than a chance at a better purchase.

  Source: guildrun.wiki Economy — https://guildrun.wiki/systems/economy/

evidenceType: confirmed-fact
sourceRefs: [S04]

heading: Why Reroll Addiction Happens
body: |
  Each reroll feels productive ("I'm taking action!") but it's pure gambling. The reroll doesn't improve your odds — it just gives you another independent roll. If there isn't a specific hero/item you're targeting, and the current shop already has something usable, buy it and move on.

evidenceType: editorial-recommendation
sourceRefs: [S04]

heading: How to Fix Reroll Addiction
body: |
  Set a hard limit of 2 rerolls per shop. If you haven't found what you need after two, buy the best available option and save your Shards for the next shop (or the Auction House). The shop will refresh for free after the next fight anyway.

### Mistake 2: Ignoring Rush

heading: Rush Mechanics You're Missing
body: |
  **The mistake**: Playing every fight the same way, never trying to end fights in the Rush window.

  **What it costs**: Rush effects (available to Warrior, Vanguard, Assassin, Duelist) give you bonuses in the first N seconds of combat. According to guildrun.wiki, which describes its reference data as extracted from the current demo build: "The effect is live only during the first N seconds of combat (shown as Rush (N)), then shuts off." Faster kills = more surviving heroes = more Shards. Dead heroes earn zero Shards, reducing your spending power for the rest of the run.

  Source: guildrun.wiki Glossary — https://guildrun.wiki/systems/glossary/#rush

evidenceType: confirmed-fact
sourceRefs: [S02]

heading: Why Beginners Ignore Rush
body: |
  Beginners focus on "just surviving the fight" rather than "winning the fight efficiently." The difference between a 3-survivor win and a 1-survivor win is real Shard income that compounds across your run.

evidenceType: editorial-recommendation
sourceRefs: []

heading: How to Start Using Rush
body: |
  If you have Rush-class heroes, position them aggressively (front row, closest to enemies) and give them items that maximize the Rush window (Attack Speed, Crit, Attack). The goal isn't just to win — it's to win fast with everyone alive.

### Mistake 3: Bad Freeze Timing

heading: Freeze Too Early, Freeze Too Late
body: |
  **The mistake**: Either freezing a shop for a mediocre hero you don't actually need, or not freezing when a crucial duplicate appears.

  **Bad freeze (too early)**: You freeze a rank C duplicate on Act 1 for a hero you might replace. Now you've locked your shop for the next fight — you can't buy anything else even if you wanted to. And when you come back, the upgrade from C to B might not matter if you find a rank A replacement in the Auction House two fights later.

  **Missed freeze (too late)**: You see a rank-up for your core S-tier carry but you're 2 Shards short. You don't freeze, hoping it'll come back. It doesn't. You just passed up a guaranteed +25% HP, +30 Defense, +25 Attack, +20 Attack Speed bonus. That mistake compounds across the entire run.

  **The fix**: Only freeze when the item is (a) a duplicate for a hero you're committed to keeping, and (b) you'll have the Shards after one more fight. If both conditions aren't met, let the shop roll.

  Source: guildrun.wiki Economy — https://guildrun.wiki/systems/economy/

evidenceType: editorial-recommendation

### Mistake 4: Item-Class Mismatch

heading: Wrong Items on Wrong Heroes
body: |
  **The mistake**: Equipping the highest-rarity item on whoever has an open slot, without checking if the stats match.

  **What it costs**: An S-rank Magic item on a Warrior (who scales with Attack, not Magic) is essentially a wasted slot. Rarity only matters if the stats are useful. A C-rank Attack item on a Warrior will contribute more damage than an S-rank Magic item.

  **Quick class-item matching**:
  - Warrior → Attack, Crit
  - Assassin → Attack Speed, Crit, OmniVamp
  - Duelist → Attack Speed, OmniVamp
  - Tank → Defense, HP, Shields
  - Vanguard → HP, Defense
  - Mage → Magic, Mana Regen
  - Mystic → Mana Regen, Magic

  **The fix**: Before equipping, ask whether this hero scales with these stats. If unsure, check the hero's class. Attack classes use Attack/Crit/Attack Speed. Magic classes use Magic/Mana Regen. Defense classes use Defense/HP.

### Mistake 5: Starting on Too High Difficulty

heading: The SS Wall Is Real
body: |
  **The mistake**: Unlocking a new difficulty and immediately trying it, before you can consistently clear the current one.

  In the community: multiple players report hitting a wall at SS rank where even the first boss becomes unbeatable. One player wrote: "After I got to SS I cant even beat the first boss anymore on the easiest difficulty. NOTHING but common garbage items dropping." — Sidrandom, July 21 2026. This post got 7 replies indicating a shared experience.

  Source: Steam community — https://steamcommunity.com/app/3669200/discussions/0/618435289300111365/

evidenceType: community-report
sourceRefs: [S05]

heading: How to Handle Difficulty Progression
body: |
  Each difficulty tier adds a compounding modifier (starting HP reduction, Shard penalties, item slot gating, boss relic buffs). Jumping up before you're ready means you'll die without learning why — you can't tell whether you lost from a strategic mistake or because the game is just harder.

  Clear each difficulty tier at least 2-3 times before moving up. You're looking for consistency, not a lucky run. If you can win 3 out of 5 runs on your current tier, you're ready for the next. If you're barely scraping by with 1-2 survivors, drop back and refine your strategy.

### Mistake 6: Ignoring Your Reserve

heading: Bench Management — It's Not a Storage Closet
body: |
  **The mistake**: Filling your reserve with heroes you bought "just in case" and never using them.

  **The fix**: Your reserve should contain heroes you plan to swap in or heroes with Backup abilities that work from the bench. According to guildrun.wiki, the Backup keyword means: "The effect also works while the hero is in reserve (on the bench)." If a hero has been sitting in reserve for 3 acts without contributing, sell it and free up the slot. A full reserve (roster cap of 6 heroes) prevents you from buying heroes you actually want.

  Source: guildrun.wiki Economy — https://guildrun.wiki/systems/economy/

evidenceType: confirmed-fact
sourceRefs: [S04, S02]

### Mistake 7: The Duelist Animation Trap

heading: Duelist Skills — When Abilities Get You Killed
body: |
  **The mistake**: Not realizing that Duelist skill animations can get your hero killed.

  **What happens**: Duelists have extremely high Attack Speed, which means fast mana generation and frequent abilities. But during the ability animation, the Duelist stops auto-attacking — which means it also stops healing (from OmniVamp) and stops generating mana. In a high-damage fight, that pause is long enough for the enemy to kill the Duelist.

  A Steam community member (Baprika, July 20 2026) reported this directly: "Duelist skills stop attack speed → they don't heal during animation → can die during skill use. It should feel like a great moment for a skill to activate, but sometimes it just kills your hero."

  Community thread: https://steamcommunity.com/app/3669200/discussions/0/618435289300111361/

evidenceType: community-report
sourceRefs: [S09]

heading: How to Handle Duelist Animations
body: |
  This appears to be a design consideration rather than a confirmed bug. If your Duelist is dying mid-animation, check whether it has enough HP/Defense to survive the animation window. Consider adding a Tank to absorb damage while the Duelist casts. The high Attack Speed → frequent abilities tradeoff is intentional — the fix is team composition, not avoiding skills.
