## directAnswer

Relics are run-defining modifiers that change how your heroes, items, economy, and enemy encounters behave across a Guildrun dungeon run. According to the Steam store page, there are over 300 relics available (L001). Unlike hero items, relics apply globally to your entire team and often force a strategic pivot — you either build into the relic's condition or you ignore it. The developer FAQ (L002) confirms that the game is designed around adaptation, meaning no single relic strategy works for every run. Rush relics reward fast clears, survival relics extend your sustain, scaling relics grow in power over floors, economy relics manipulate gold and shop prices, and trigger relics fire on specific combat events. The most common mistakes are over-valuing high-rarity relics that do not match your build, breaking a working synergy to chase a relic, and trusting community claims without testing context. This guide explains each relic category, how to evaluate relics, when to pivot, and what the community gets wrong.

### What Relics Change in a Run

heading: What Relics Change in a Run
body: |
  Relics are persistent modifications that alter the rules of your entire dungeon run. They can:
  - Modify hero stats (attack, defense, speed, HP) across the whole team.
  - Change how gold is earned, spent, or multiplied.
  - Alter enemy behavior, spawning patterns, or floor modifiers.
  - Introduce conditional bonuses (e.g., "If no hero falls below 50% HP, gain +20% damage").
  - Stack with other relics to create powerful synergies.
  - Define late-run scaling by providing exponential benefits.

  The Steam store page (L001) advertises "300+ relics," making relic evaluation one of the deepest strategic layers in Guildrun. Each run presents a different set of relic choices, and mastering when to take, skip, or pivot around a relic is central to climbing difficulty tiers.

### Relics vs Hero Items

heading: Relics vs Hero Items
body: |
  A common confusion for new players is the difference between relics and hero items.
  
  - **Relics** are global. One relic affects your entire team for the rest of the run. You choose relics at specific decision points (draft nodes, shops, events).
  - **Hero Items** are per-hero. Each hero has equipment slots, and items only affect that specific hero's stats or abilities.
  - **Persistence**: Relics persist for the entire run once acquired. Some hero items may be swapped, sold, or consumed.
  - **Strategic weight**: Relics often define your run direction. Items support that direction.

  When evaluating whether to buy a relic or a hero item, ask: does this relic change my win condition? If yes, prioritize the relic. If you just need raw stats to survive the next floor, a hero item may be the safer short-term choice.

### Starting from Failure Condition

heading: Starting from Failure Condition
body: |
  An effective way to evaluate relics is to ask the inverse question: "What causes my run to fail, and does this relic prevent that failure?"

  Common failure conditions and matching relic strategies:
  - **Frontline dies too fast** → Look for survival relics that boost defense, HP, or healing.
  - **Cannot clear enemies before the timer** → Look for rush relics that reward speed and burst damage.
  - **Run out of gold for key items** → Economy relics that increase gold gain or reduce shop prices.
  - **Late-game scaling falls off** → Scaling relics that grow in power over floors.
  - **Specific enemy types wall you** → Trigger relics that counter those enemy mechanics.

  The developer FAQ (L002) emphasizes that Guildrun is about adaptation. Starting your relic evaluation from what kills you — rather than from a tier list — is the mindset the game rewards.

### Rush Relics

heading: Rush Relics
body: |
  Rush relics reward fast floor completion and aggressive play. They typically provide bonuses when enemies are defeated quickly, when the party avoids taking damage, or when floors are cleared under a time threshold.

  Key characteristics:
  - High tempo, short-term payoff.
  - Synergize with high-damage, low-durability team compositions.
  - Risk becoming dead weight in longer fights or boss encounters if the condition cannot be met.
  - Best used on lower-to-mid difficulties where enemies die quickly.

  Evaluate rush relics by asking: "Can my team consistently trigger this condition across all remaining floors?" If the answer is "probably not on the boss floor," the relic may be a trap.

### Survival Relics

heading: Survival Relics
body: |
  Survival relics extend your team's staying power — more HP, defense, healing, shields, or damage reduction. They are the backbone of stall and sustain team compositions.

  Key characteristics:
  - Reduce the risk of sudden hero deaths.
  - Enable safer exploration of risk/reward nodes (elite fights, curse rooms).
  - Synergize with healing-focused heroes and defensive items.
  - May slow down clear speed if over-stacked — you survive but time out.

  Survival relics are rarely a wrong choice on higher difficulties, but over-prioritizing them can create a "survive but fail" problem where your team cannot output enough damage to clear the floor or boss in time.

### Scaling Relics

heading: Scaling Relics
body: |
  Scaling relics grow in power as the run progresses — per-floor bonuses, per-kill stacking, or multipliers that increase with relic count. They are the strongest late-game relics and essential for Endless mode.

  Key characteristics:
  - Weak when first acquired; become dominant later.
  - Require early commitment — taking a scaling relic on floor 1 pays off on floor 20.
  - Synergize with each other (multiple scaling relics create multiplicative power curves).
  - The risk: you may die before the scaling kicks in. On high difficulties, this trade-off is sharp.

  A general rule: take at most one scaling relic before floor 5 unless you have strong early-game heroes. After floor 10, scaling relics are almost always correct picks.
evidenceType: editorial-recommendation
sourceRefs: [L001, L002]

### Economy Relics

heading: Economy Relics
body: |
  Economy relics manipulate gold — increased gold drops, reduced shop prices, bonus gold on floor clear, interest mechanics, or gold-to-stat conversions.

  Key characteristics:
  - Indirect power — they do not make you stronger immediately but enable more purchases later.
  - Compound value: an early economy relic can fund 2-3 extra hero items or relics by endgame.
  - Risk: taking too many economy relics leaves you stat-deficient for upcoming fights.
  - Evaluate by calculating the "break-even floor": at which floor does the extra gold translate into stats greater than what a direct-stat relic would have provided?

  Economy relics are strongest when the shop offers rerolls or when you are building around expensive synergy items.
evidenceType: editorial-recommendation
sourceRefs: [L001]

### Trigger Relics

heading: Trigger Relics
body: |
  Trigger relics fire automatically when specific conditions are met in combat — on-kill, on-hit, on-heal, on-dodge, on-critical, on-floor-start, on-boss-phase.

  Key characteristics:
  - Power varies wildly depending on how often the trigger condition occurs.
  - Some triggers are "win-more" (only fire when you are already winning).
  - Others are "comeback" mechanics (fire when a hero is low HP).
  - Evaluate trigger relics by estimating trigger frequency: if a relic triggers "on hero death," how often do your heroes die? If rarely, the relic is wasted.
  
  The best trigger relics are those whose conditions align with your team's natural combat pattern — they trigger passively without changing how you play.

### When to Pivot Around a Relic

heading: When to Pivot Around a Relic
body: |
  Pivoting means changing your team composition, item build, or overall strategy to maximize a relic's value. Pivoting is correct when:

  1. The relic is a top-rarity relic that defines a win condition on its own.
  2. Your current build direction has not heavily committed resources (heroes, items, relics) yet.
  3. The relic directly counters the primary threat for your current difficulty tier.
  4. You have identified that your current path cannot win — the relic offers a new path.

  Pivoting is risky and should not be done lightly. Every pivot costs resources: items you bought for the old direction become suboptimal, hero upgrades may lose synergy, and you may lack the gold to re-equip.

### When Not to Break a Working Build

heading: When Not to Break a Working Build
body: |
  The most common relic mistake is abandoning a proven strategy for an exciting new relic. Signs you should NOT pivot:

  1. Your current team is consistently clearing floors without losses.
  2. You have already invested in 3+ relics that synergize with each other.
  3. The new relic's condition contradicts your team's core mechanic (e.g., a rush relic when your team is built for sustain).
  4. You are past floor 15 — late-game pivoting rarely pays off before the run ends.

  A useful heuristic: if your current build can reliably clear the next 3 floors, do not pivot. If you are barely surviving each floor, a pivot may be necessary.

### Why Rarity Alone Is Not Enough

heading: Why Rarity Alone Is Not Enough
body: |
  Higher rarity does not mean better. Guildrun relics are designed so that rarity indicates uniqueness and power ceiling, not guaranteed effectiveness.

  - A common-rarity relic that matches your build is often better than a legendary relic that does not.
  - High-rarity relics frequently have restrictive conditions. If you cannot meet the condition, the relic provides zero value.
  - Some lower-rarity relics have consistent, unconditional value that outperforms conditional high-rarity relics over a full run.

  New players tend to over-value legendary and epic relics. Experienced players evaluate relics by condition match first, rarity second.

### Evaluating Community Relic Claims

heading: Evaluating Community Relic Claims
body: |
  Community relic tier lists and "best relic" discussions are common on Steam and Discord, but they require careful evaluation:

  1. **Check the difficulty context.** A relic that is S-tier on normal difficulty may be unusable on SS.
  2. **Check the patch version.** Relics are rebalanced; a claim from a previous patch may be outdated.
  3. **Identify the assumed team composition.** "Best relic" claims usually assume a specific build archetype.
  4. **Look for corroboration.** One player's experience does not make a relic universally good.
  5. **Test in your own runs.** The developer FAQ (L002) confirms that Guildrun is designed for adaptation — what works for one player may not work for your playstyle.

### Common Mistakes

heading: Common Mistakes
body: |
  - **Chasing rarity**: Picking the highest-rarity relic without reading the condition.
  - **Ignoring condition mismatch**: Taking a relic whose trigger condition your team never meets.
  - **Too many scaling relics early**: Dying on floor 8 because all relics are scaling and none provide immediate power.
  - **No economy plan**: Buying relics without considering how to fund future purchases.
  - **Blindly following tier lists**: Copying community relic rankings without understanding the context.
  - **Pivoting too late**: Trying to switch build direction after floor 15.
  - **Over-stacking one category**: All survival relics with no damage output leads to timeout losses.

### FAQ

heading: FAQ
body: |
  **Q: How many relics can I have in one run?**
  A: There is no hard cap, but relic acquisition opportunities are limited to draft nodes, shops, and events. The Steam store confirms 300+ relics exist (L001), so each run sees only a small fraction.

  **Q: Can I remove or replace a relic?**
  A: Based on current information, relics are generally permanent once acquired. Some specific relics or events may allow swapping, but this is unconfirmed.

  **Q: Should I always take a relic when offered?**
  A: No. Sometimes skipping a relic is correct — especially if all options have negative conditions or contradict your build.

  **Q: Are there relics that affect the entire party's class/role?**
  A: Some relics provide bonuses that benefit specific hero roles (tanks, healers, DPS). These relics are stronger when your team leans into that role.

  **Q: Do relics affect Endless mode differently?**
  A: In Endless, scaling relics become disproportionately valuable because the run length is much longer. Economy relics also gain value because more floors mean more shops.
